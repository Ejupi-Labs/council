# Data Model: Recovery and Retention

This is a design contract for schema version 7, not a claim that the records or transitions exist. Existing 006 task, graph, attempt, event, artifact, capacity, workflow, delivery and receipt records remain authoritative and must be migrated without mutation or loss. IDs and revisions follow existing strict lossless public conventions. All fields below are required unless explicitly optional; bounds and wire shape belong in the interface contracts.

## Pause scope and checkpoint

- **PauseScope**: `scopeKind` is `task` for a standalone task, `member` for a graph-owned task, or `graph` for a whole group; it binds the corresponding task/graph/node identities and current authority tuple, plus `pauseId`, `requestId`, control revision, accepting owner epoch, state (`pausing`, `paused`, `resumed`, `cancelled`, `failed`), accepted timestamp and optional checkpoint ID. A member scope retains its exact graph membership, controls, dependency and shared-budget authority and never creates standalone authority.
- **Checkpoint**: ID, pause ID, scope/version and control revisions, task/instruction versions, exact completed and unfinished execution identities, relevant retained source/input/attempt/terminal/effect references, current budget/window authority, durable evidence status and next action. It refers to originals; it does not clone or relabel completed results.
- **Invariants**: A pause and all admissions/continuations race within one serialized authority transaction. No new scoped work crosses a committed pause. Existing admitted work may finish subject to its captured authority, Stop and failure precedence. Only a durable known checkpoint is `paused`; unresolved effects cannot be relabelled settled. Restart observes the pause, never dispatches. Explicit Resume has an independent request/receipt, rechecks current revisions, inputs, owner, budgets, stops, dependencies and other fences, and never recreates completed work or units. A stale resume conflicts; same request and input replay its original receipt.
- **Transitions**: active → pausing on accepted pause; pausing → paused after admitted work settles and checkpoint is durable; paused → resumed only via explicit authorized resume. Group pause covers every member; member pause affects that member and its dependency constraints without silently pausing unrelated siblings. Overlapping member/group scopes keep independent pause identities and checkpoints: resuming a member cannot bypass an active group pause, and resuming a group cannot release an independent member pause. Only work clear of every applicable pause and other control may be admitted. Failure, Stop, persistence loss or ownership uncertainty retain their own state and precedence; no transition erases those outcomes.

## Recovery incident and evidence

- **RecoveryIncident**: incident ID, scope, original owner epoch, optional attempt/effect ID, retained claim identities, primary failure/cancellation reference, state (`unresolved` or `reconciled`), uncertainty categories, revision and retained audit IDs.
- **ReconciliationObservation**: ID, incident ID/revision, current authenticated owner, observed exact native ownership and complete-tree cleanup evidence, effect journal and retained-evidence identities/digests, outcome and reason. Do not store credentials, client assertions as proof, or raw process handles as portable identity.
- **ReconciliationOperation**: exact request and fingerprint, target incident/revision, observed proof references, result, retained/released claim IDs and remaining blocker summary. Same ID + same input returns the saved result; mismatched input conflicts.
- **Positive transition**: A still-held Council-owned process object reports verified cleanup of the exact complete owned tree, and all relevant actual effects and retained evidence are durably complete and consistent. The owner retains auditable proof; in a transaction, validate target/revision/claim, check all remaining claims/incidents/policy, mark only this incident reconciled and release only its verified-safe claim. Recompute scope/global fencing; another unresolved blocker continues to fence. No dispatch occurs as part of reconciliation.
- **Negative transition**: Missing process/tree proof, mismatched owner/effect/claim, partial evidence or uncertain persistence leaves the incident and its claim unresolved/fenced. A PID, elapsed time, user assertion, old owner disappearance or missing output is insufficient. After owner crash, do not imply a vanished process handle can be recovered; unresolved may be permanent without an independently verifiable proof mechanism defined by the contract.

## Shared controls and migrations

All new public mutations use existing exact-request receipts and fingerprints, scope and optimistic revisions. Receipt replay precedes current-owner and new-effect checks; a reused identity with different inputs conflicts. IDs, counts and cursors remain bounded and lossless; UI/CLI observe the same truth and do not invent states. Pause and reconciliation never bypass current global/graph/account/budget controls.

Migration 6→7 requires a frozen populated v6 fixture, exact schema/index/constraint validation before and after, preservation of 001–006 records and receipt bytes, retention of every unresolved incident and claim, transaction rollback on error and refusal of unknown schema. Reopen/restart tests verify the new tables without accessing legacy state or vendor credentials.

## Deferred next slice

Export, backup, restore and cleanup entities and filesystem effects are outside 007 and belong to the immediately following feature. Arbitrary user text/files currently lack a proven credential-free provenance mechanism. That future feature must address this production gap and clearly distinguish any metadata-only profile from complete restorable project content; this is neither implemented here nor an obstacle to the scoped pause/reconciliation work.
